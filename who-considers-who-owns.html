CONSIDERING CRYPTO?

Most crypto studies look at ownership — risking important differences between non-owners who would not consider owning vs. those who would consider but haven't acted yet.

Cryptocurrency is a rapidly expanding, multi-trillion dollar landscape spanning sociopolitical, psychological, technological, and financial domains. Understanding who considers and owns crypto matters for communicators, policymakers, educators, and researchers interested in studying and/or reaching these groups.
Most research focuses only on owners. Prior work that accounts for considerers uses methods that don't allow direct comparison between all three groups. As a result, we know little about broader patterns of individual-level crypto adoption in the United States.
According to the Theory of Planned Behavior (Ajzen 1985; 2020; Fishbein and Ajzen 1975), people must think about and form intentions to engage in a behavior before taking action. However, thinking positively about a behavior doesn't always lead to intention, and intentions don't guarantee follow through.
We argue that considerers and owners may both differ from rejectors — but considerers aren't necessarily like owners in all ways or to the same degree.
CRYPTO ISN'T JUST "IN" OR "OUT"

We asked respondents if they owned crypto and, if not, whether they would consider it. In all three studies, ~20% would consider owning — suggesting considerers are a real, measurable group.

MEASURES & MODELING

We use a multivariate model that simultaneously estimates the probability of being in the rejecting, considering, or owning group, and repeat our analysis using three independent samples.

Multivariate, generalized ordered logistic regression (partial proportional odds) models — run separately for each of three independent, nationally-representative samples of US adults.
A three-level outcome capturing each crypto adoption stage: rejectors (do not own and would not consider), considerers (do not own but would consider), and owners (currently own cryptocurrency). Each respondent is classified into exactly one group.

WHAT OUR FINDINGS MEAN FOR...

Our findings have significant implications across multiple domains.

The "considerer" is arguably the most actionable audience — potentially open to adoption, but hasn't acted yet. No single approach will appeal to all: efforts to reduce uncertainty may attract considerers, but backfire with owners who think differently about institutional disruption and risk.
~20% of US adults would consider owning crypto — significant latent demand. Consideration may depend on pending regulation, perceived risk, and legitimacy signals. Policymakers will likely need to balance mainstream and alternative frames.
Looking only at ownership obscures variation among non-owners. By accounting for considerers, we find certain factors (e.g., ideology and tech attitudes) do matter for crypto adoption — just not at the ownership stage.
Considering and owning rates suggest US crypto adoption is at or nearing the "early majority" stage. Our findings align with decades of attitude-behavior research and should be broadly applicable.
Most respondents consider or own Bitcoin, but a sizable share aren't interested at all. Our evidence indicates a broad orientation toward digital assets in general, rather than a specific asset type.
